# Dallas, PA Garden Checklist — March through Harvest
### Zone 6a | Back Mountain, Luzerne County
**Last Frost:** ~May 15 | **First Fall Frost:** ~October 15 | **Growing Season:** ~150 days
**Soil Notes:** Glacially-deposited loamy-clay, naturally acidic (pH 5.5–6.5). Amend with compost
each season. Avoid mixing in sand alone — it can create concrete-like hardpan. Test soil pH
every 2–3 years (Penn State Extension offers low-cost soil testing).

---

## Your Crops + Suggested Additions

| Crop | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Snap Peas | Cool-season, direct sow | Sow early — they love cold soil |
| Sweet Peppers | Warm-season, transplant | Need the longest indoor head start |
| Roma Tomatoes | Warm-season, transplant | Determinate — great for canning |
| Strawberries | Perennial | Plant bare root in April; returns yearly |
| Carrots | Cool-season, direct sow | Needs loose, deeply worked soil — amend well |
| Cosmos | Warm-season, direct sow | Sow after May 15; self-seeds — comes back each year |
| Scarlet Flax | Cool-season, direct sow | Direct sow early April; loves cold, dislikes transplanting |
| Iris | Perennial (rhizome) | Plant rhizomes July–September; blooms May–June |
| Sunflowers | Warm-season, direct sow | Direct sow May 15+; plant in succession every 2 weeks for longer bloom |
| **Cucumbers** *(suggested)* | Warm-season, direct sow | Easy, prolific, thrives in PA summers |
| **Zucchini** *(suggested)* | Warm-season, direct sow | High yield, low effort — 2 plants = plenty |
| **Bush Beans** *(suggested)* | Warm-season, direct sow | No trellising, succession-plant every 3 weeks |
| **Basil** *(suggested)* | Warm-season, transplant | Companion to tomatoes; deters aphids |
| **Lettuce/Spinach** *(suggested)* | Cool-season, direct sow | Quick spring + fall crop; fills early/late gaps |

## Weekly Focus Areas

### MARCH

#### Week 1 — March 15–21: Seed Starting + Planning
> *You are here. Soil is still too cold and wet to work outdoors.*

- [ ] **Start sweet peppers indoors** (8–10 weeks before May 15 — start NOW or this week at latest)
  - Use a heat mat (70–80°F for germination), full-spectrum grow light
  - Sow 2–3 seeds per cell, thin to strongest seedling
- [ ] **Start Roma tomatoes indoors** (6–8 weeks before May 15 — start by March 20)
  - Same setup as peppers; they germinate faster (~7 days vs 14 for peppers)
- [ ] **Order/purchase:**
  - Snap pea seeds (Sugar Snap or Mammoth Melting Sugar do well in zone 6)
  - Bare-root strawberry plants (Earliglow or Honeoye — June-bearing; Seascape for everbearing)
  - Carrot seeds (Danvers 126 or Nantes half-long — both tolerate heavier soils)
  - Cucumber, zucchini, bush bean seeds if adding
- [ ] **Submit a soil test** to Penn State Extension (psu.edu/dept/soils) if not done in last 2 years
- [ ] **Gather supplies:** compost, pea trellis/netting, row cover/frost cloth, tomato cages

#### Week 2 — March 22–28: Indoor Care + Bed Prep Planning
> *Overnight temps still in the 20s–30s. Watch for late snowstorms — common in the Back Mountain.*

- [ ] Monitor pepper and tomato seedlings — keep lights 2–3 inches above leaves
- [ ] Water seedlings from the bottom to prevent damping off
- [ ] Sketch your garden layout — place taller crops (tomatoes, cucumbers) on north/east end
  so they don't shade shorter crops (carrots, strawberries, lettuce)
- [ ] Plan pea trellis location — snap peas need 4–6 ft of vertical support
- [ ] If soil is workable on a dry day: **turn compost into beds** (2–3 inches worked in 6–8 inches deep)
- [ ] Start basil indoors if adding (4–6 weeks before transplant date)

#### Week 3 — March 29–April 4: First Direct Sow Window
> *Soil temp goal: 40°F+ for peas. Check with a cheap soil thermometer.*

- [ ] **Direct sow snap peas** as soon as soil can be worked (they tolerate light frost)
  - Sow 1 inch deep, 2 inches apart; inoculate seeds with rhizobium innoculant for better yield
  - Install trellis at planting time (roots hate disturbance later)
- [ ] **Direct sow lettuce and spinach** in a separate bed or container (soil temp 40°F+)
- [ ] Thin pepper and tomato seedlings if multiple sprouted per cell — keep the strongest one
- [ ] Fertilize indoor seedlings with diluted liquid fertilizer (half-strength balanced 10-10-10)
- [ ] **Direct sow Scarlet Flax** outdoors — it tolerates frost and prefers cool soil (sow March 29–April 10)
  - Scatter seeds thinly on prepared soil, press in lightly — needs light to germinate, do not cover deeply
  - Blooms in about 6–8 weeks; will self-sow if you let some go to seed
- [ ] **Check Back Mountain weather forecasts** — late March cold snaps can still hit hard

---

### APRIL

#### Week 4 — April 5–11: Soil Amendment + Strawberry Planting
> *Avg temps: 40–55°F. Ground workable but still cool. Last frost still 5+ weeks away.*

- [ ] **Amend all planting beds** with 2–3 inches of compost worked into top 8 inches
  - Add lime if pH is below 6.0 (from your soil test) — peppers and tomatoes prefer 6.0–6.8
  - Carrots need especially loose, deep (12 inches) worked soil — remove rocks and break up clods
- [ ] **Plant bare-root strawberries** as soon as soil is workable (~April 5–15 is ideal)
  - Set crown at soil level — not too deep (crowns rot) or too shallow (roots dry out)
  - Space 12–18 inches apart in rows 24 inches wide
  - Water in well; mulch lightly with straw
- [ ] Snap peas should be sprouting — check germination; resow any bare patches
- [ ] **Purchase iris rhizomes** if you don't already have them — note: irises planted this year will bloom next May/June; existing established irises will bloom this spring
- [ ] Harden off lettuce/spinach seedlings if started indoors by setting outside in a sheltered spot

#### Week 5 — April 12–18: Succession Sowing + Seedling Care
> *Avg temps: 45–60°F. Last frost ~4 weeks away.*

- [ ] **Direct sow carrots** (3–4 weeks before last frost is fine — they're slow to germinate)
  - Mix seeds with dry sand for even distribution — carrots are tiny
  - Keep the seed bed consistently moist until germination (10–21 days)
  - Cover with burlap or row cover to retain moisture and warmth
- [ ] Check strawberry plants — water if dry, watch for frost heaving (re-firm roots if lifted)
- [ ] Begin **hardening off tomato and pepper seedlings** on mild days (50°F+, no wind)
  - Start with 1 hour outside in dappled shade; add 1 hour each day over 7–10 days
  - Bring in if frost is predicted — no exceptions until after May 15
- [ ] Pinch off any flowers on pepper seedlings to redirect energy to root development

#### Week 6 — April 19–25: Beds Ready, Peas Growing
> *Avg temps: 50–65°F. Snap peas should be 4–6 inches tall.*

- [ ] **Continue hardening off** tomatoes and peppers — they should now tolerate 4–6 hours outside
- [ ] **Mark carrot rows** with sticks — germination is slow and it's easy to accidentally weed them out
- [ ] Check snap peas — guide tendrils to trellis as needed
- [ ] Prepare warm-season beds: lay soaker hose or drip irrigation if using (much easier before transplants)
- [ ] Strawberry check: remove runners this season for stronger plants and better fruit next year
- [ ] Sow a second round of lettuce/spinach for succession harvest

#### Week 7 — April 26–May 2: Prep for Transplant Week
> *Avg temps: 52–67°F. Last frost 2 weeks away — don't rush transplanting.*

- [ ] Finalize hardening off — tomatoes and peppers should now tolerate full sun outdoors during the day
- [ ] Install tomato cages or stakes in beds now (easier without plants in the way)
- [ ] **Direct sow cucumbers and zucchini seeds indoors** (2–3 weeks before transplant date)
  - Or plan to direct sow outdoors after May 15
- [ ] Direct sow bush beans in a tray to start, or wait and direct sow outdoors May 15+
- [ ] Check carrot germination — thin to 2 inches apart once seedlings reach 2 inches tall
- [ ] Keep row cover handy — Back Mountain late April frosts happen

---

### MAY

#### Week 8 — May 3–9: Final Frost Watch
> *Avg temps: 55–70°F. Last frost window: May 10–20. Do NOT transplant warm-season crops yet.*

- [ ] **Keep frost cloth/row covers ready** — this is the highest-risk week
- [ ] Tomato and pepper seedlings should be fully hardened off by now
- [ ] Snap peas climbing and flowering — great sign, first pods forming soon
- [ ] Strawberry plants flowering — this is normal for Year 1; June-bearing will fruit in June
- [ ] Water carrot bed carefully — seeds are still fragile
- [ ] Pre-wet planting holes for transplants with diluted liquid fertilizer ("starter solution")

#### Week 9 — May 10–16: Last Frost Threshold
> *Watch your 10-day forecast carefully. May 10–15 is the average last frost for Dallas PA.*

- [ ] **Do NOT transplant peppers or tomatoes until after your last forecast frost**
- [ ] If a frost is predicted after you've transplanted, cover with row cover overnight — peppers die at 32°F
- [ ] Harvest snap peas as pods fill and are plump — do not let them over-mature (they get starchy)
- [ ] Begin harvesting lettuce and spinach — cut outer leaves, let center regrow
- [ ] Thin carrots to 3–4 inches apart (thinnings are edible — use in salads)
- [ ] Strawberry check — water and mulch well

#### Week 10 — May 17–23: Transplant Week
> *Last frost has passed. This is the most important planting week of the year.*

- [ ] **Transplant sweet peppers** into garden
  - Space 18 inches apart; plant at same depth as pot
  - Water in with diluted fertilizer; stake if tall or floppy
- [ ] **Transplant Roma tomatoes** into garden
  - Plant deep — bury 2/3 of the stem; roots grow along the buried stem
  - Space 24–30 inches apart; attach to cage or stake immediately
- [ ] **Direct sow cucumbers** (or transplant starts if started indoors)
  - Plant at base of trellis or fence; they climb aggressively
- [ ] **Direct sow zucchini** (2–3 seeds per hill, thin to 1 strongest plant)
- [ ] **Direct sow bush beans** — first planting
- [ ] **Direct sow cosmos** — scatter in a dedicated bed or among vegetables; thin to 12 inches apart
  - They bloom in 7–8 weeks and attract pollinators that help your vegetables
- [ ] **Direct sow sunflowers** — first planting
  - Plant 1 inch deep, 6 inches apart (thin to 12–24 inches); full sun required
  - Sow a second round June 1 for a later flush of blooms
- [ ] Mulch everything with 2–3 inches of straw or shredded leaves (moisture + weed control)

#### Week 11 — May 24–31: Establishment Week
> *Focus on helping new transplants establish roots, not grow fast.*

- [ ] Water transplants deeply every 2–3 days (1 inch total per week) — deep, infrequent watering
  grows deeper roots than frequent shallow watering
- [ ] Watch for **cutworms** — collars around tomato and pepper stems at soil level help
- [ ] Watch for **aphids** on peppers — spray with water or insecticidal soap if found
- [ ] Snap peas: harvest regularly — the more you pick, the more the plant produces
- [ ] Succession sow second round of bush beans (sow every 3 weeks through July for continuous harvest)
- [ ] Remove strawberry runners — or pin them to a new bed location to propagate new plants

---

### JUNE

#### Week 12 — June 1–7: Early Summer Growth Surge
> *Avg temps: 62–78°F. Plants take off. Watering becomes critical.*

- [ ] Fertilize tomatoes and peppers — use a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) or tomato-specific fertilizer
  - Switch to lower-nitrogen, higher-phosphorus/potassium once flowering begins
- [ ] **First strawberry harvest** (June-bearing varieties) — pick when fully red, every 1–2 days
- [ ] Snap peas still producing — harvest daily at this stage
- [ ] Thin basil; pinch flower buds off basil to keep leaves coming
- [ ] Tomatoes: remove suckers (shoots between main stem and branch) if you want tidier, airflow-improved plants
  - Note: Romas are determinate — light pruning only, don't remove all growth

#### Week 13 — June 8–14: Pest and Disease Watch
> *Humid PA summers begin — watch for early blight and fungal issues on tomatoes.*

- [ ] **Apply mulch** around tomatoes to prevent soil splash (primary cause of early blight)
- [ ] Check tomato lower leaves for early blight (yellowing with dark bull's-eye spots) — remove immediately
- [ ] Watch for **tomato hornworms** — large green caterpillars, handpick or use Bt (Bacillus thuringiensis)
- [ ] Strawberry harvest peaking — wash, freeze, or jam excess
- [ ] Cucumbers flowering — hand-pollinate if needed, or ensure pollinators can access them
- [ ] Bush beans growing — first sowing should be knee-high

#### Week 14 — June 15–21: Mid-June Maintenance
> *Snap peas will decline in heat. Plan for pulling and replanting bed.*

- [ ] Snap peas: production will slow dramatically as temps hit 70°F+ consistently
  - Enjoy last harvests; plan to pull plants in late June/early July
- [ ] **Manage strawberry runners** — keep bed from overcrowding; pin 1–2 runners per plant if propagating
- [ ] Fertilize cucumbers and zucchini — they are heavy feeders
- [ ] Watch for **powdery mildew** on zucchini leaves — improve air circulation, avoid overhead watering
- [ ] Water deeply in morning, not evening — wet foliage overnight promotes disease

#### Week 15 — June 22–30: First Zucchini + Cucumber Harvest
> *Avg temps: 68–82°F. Summer in full swing.*

- [ ] **Harvest zucchini at 6–8 inches** — they turn into baseball bats overnight; check daily
- [ ] **First cucumber harvest** as fruits reach 6–8 inches (slice) or 2–3 inches (pickling)
- [ ] Pull declining snap pea vines — add to compost, prep bed for fall planting
- [ ] **Sow fall snap peas in that cleared bed** (target: around Aug 15–20, so prep now, sow in August)
- [ ] Carrot check — Danvers/Nantes are typically 70–80 days; pull one test carrot to check size
- [ ] Second bush bean planting in the ground if not already done

---

### JULY

#### Week 16 — July 1–7: Peak Summer Maintenance
> *Avg temps: 70–85°F. Rain can be sporadic — consistent deep watering matters most.*

- [ ] **Water deeply** during hot stretches — 1–1.5 inches per week; more during heat waves
- [ ] Watch for **blossom end rot on Roma tomatoes** (black sunken bottom) — caused by calcium uptake
  issues, usually from inconsistent watering. Fix: water consistently, add mulch
- [ ] Tomatoes setting fruit heavily — support branches with extra ties if needed
- [ ] Fertilize peppers every 2–3 weeks
- [ ] Continue bush bean succession sowing (last sowing around July 21 for fall harvest before frost)
- [ ] Zucchini production is intense — share with neighbors or harvest small for best flavor

#### Week 17 — July 8–14: Blight Prevention on Tomatoes
> *Humid PA July = prime time for late blight. Stay ahead of it.*

- [ ] Remove and dispose of (do NOT compost) any blighted tomato leaves — bag and trash
- [ ] Apply **copper-based fungicide** as a preventive spray if blight has been an issue in past years
- [ ] Ensure tomato cages are supporting all growth — heavy fruit can break branches
- [ ] **Begin carrot harvest** if 70–80 days from sowing (approx. July 5–15 depending on sow date)
  - Pull when shoulders are visible at soil surface, ~¾ inch diameter
- [ ] Pepper plants should have multiple developing fruits — avoid over-fertilizing now (promotes leaf, not fruit)

#### Week 18 — July 15–21: First Tomato Harvest Window
> *Roma tomatoes are typically 75–80 days from transplant (~May 17 transplant = late July harvest).*

- [ ] **Watch for first Roma tomatoes to blush and ripen** — harvest when fully red and slightly soft
- [ ] **Harvest peppers** — sweet peppers can be harvested green or left to ripen to red/yellow/orange
  (fully colored peppers are sweeter and more nutritious)
- [ ] Pull any remaining spent bean plants; sow final succession if desired
- [ ] Strawberry renovation (June-bearing): Mow or cut foliage to 1 inch; thin crowns to 6–8 inches apart;
  apply compost and fertilizer to encourage new growth for next year
- [ ] **Divide and transplant iris rhizomes** if clumps are overcrowded (every 3–5 years keeps them blooming well)
  - Dig, separate, trim leaves to 6 inches, replant with top of rhizome just at soil surface — do not bury deep
- [ ] Keep weeding — weeds compete heavily for water in dry July spells

#### Week 19 — July 22–31: Process and Preserve
> *Harvests will multiply. Have a preservation plan ready.*

- [ ] Tomato harvest building — have canning jars, freezer bags ready for Romas
- [ ] Continue pepper harvest — freeze whole roasted peppers easily for winter use
- [ ] Pull zucchini plant if overwhelmed; they decline after 8–10 weeks of heavy production
- [ ] **Plan fall garden succession** in any cleared beds (kale, spinach, broccoli for fall)
- [ ] Water consistently — July heat can stress even established plants

---

### AUGUST

#### Week 20 — August 1–7: Peak Harvest + Fall Planting Begins
> *Tomatoes and peppers at peak production. Begin thinking about fall.*

- [ ] **Roma tomato harvest is full swing** — process daily; they do not hold well on the vine once ripe
- [ ] **Sow fall snap peas** (need 60–70 days; aim for harvest before Oct 15 frost)
  - Sow by August 5–10 in the bed vacated by spring peas
  - Water daily until germination — August soil is hot and dry
- [ ] **Sow fall carrots** (70–80 days; sow by Aug 1–5 for October harvest)
- [ ] **Plant fall kale and spinach transplants or direct sow** (both extremely cold-hardy, survive light frost)
- [ ] Continue cucumber and zucchini harvest; remove any yellowing/rotting fruits immediately

#### Week 21 — August 8–14: Manage End-of-Season Tomatoes
> *Start redirecting tomato energy toward ripening existing fruit.*

- [ ] **Pinch off new flower clusters** on tomatoes — any flowers set now won't ripen before frost
- [ ] Remove any damaged or disease-spotted leaves
- [ ] Check fall snap peas and carrots for germination — water consistently
- [ ] Harvest cucumbers daily — any left to go yellow signal the plant to stop producing
- [ ] Apply final fertilizer to pepper plants

#### Week 22 — August 15–21: Fall Garden Transition
> *Avg temps: 68–80°F but nights starting to cool below 60°F.*

- [ ] Fall kale/spinach beds should be established — thin if overcrowded
- [ ] Fall snap peas growing — guide to trellis
- [ ] Strawberry bed: fertilize with a balanced fertilizer to support root growth before winter
- [ ] Tomatoes: remove lower 12 inches of foliage for airflow — late blight risk remains high
- [ ] **Order garlic** (plant in October for harvest next July — fills beds after spring crops)

#### Week 23 — August 22–31: End of Summer Push
> *Harvest every day. Process and preserve aggressively.*

- [ ] Tomatoes: process Romas into sauce, paste, or frozen whole tomatoes daily
- [ ] Peppers: harvest all mature fruits; freeze, pickle, or dry excess
- [ ] Bush beans: pull final harvest; compost plants
- [ ] Check fall carrot bed — thinning to 3 inches apart if crowded
- [ ] Fall snap peas should be about knee-high and starting to set buds

---

### SEPTEMBER

#### Week 24 — September 1–7: Transition to Fall
> *Avg temps: 60–74°F. First frost still 5–6 weeks away. Production slows.*

- [ ] **Fall snap peas flowering** — harvest will begin in 2–3 weeks
- [ ] Green tomatoes still on the vine — they will ripen if nights stay above 50°F
- [ ] Continue pepper harvest — cooler nights actually improve pepper flavor
- [ ] **Harvest fall kale and spinach** — frost will improve kale flavor (converts starches to sugars)
- [ ] Garlic purchasing/prep — get hardneck varieties (Rocambole or Purple Stripe) for zone 6 PA

#### Week 25 — September 8–14: Pre-Frost Preparations
> *Watch the 10-day forecast. First frost risk increases in late September in the Back Mountain.*

- [ ] **Harvest all peppers** before a hard frost is predicted — peppers do not recover from a hard freeze
- [ ] Green tomatoes: if a frost below 32°F is forecast, **pick green tomatoes and ripen indoors**
  - Place in a single layer at room temperature, NOT in the refrigerator
- [ ] Fall snap peas: harvesting should begin
- [ ] Add straw mulch around strawberry crowns to prepare for winter
- [ ] Begin pulling spent vegetable plants and adding compost to cleared beds

#### Week 26 — September 15–21: Fall Harvest Peak
> *Avg temps: 55–68°F.*

- [ ] Harvest fall snap peas daily — same as spring, pick before they overripen
- [ ] Harvest fall carrots — check size; leave in ground through light frosts (frost sweetens them)
- [ ] Continue kale and spinach harvest — both survive frost well and improve in flavor
- [ ] **Final large tomato harvest** — process everything still on the vine
- [ ] **Plant garlic** (September 15 – October 15 is the ideal window for zone 6 PA)
  - Break bulbs into cloves; plant 2 inches deep, 6 inches apart, pointed end up
  - Mulch heavily with 4–6 inches of straw immediately after planting

#### Week 27 — September 22–30: Garden Closedown Begins
> *Nights regularly dropping to 40s°F. First frost possible any night now.*

- [ ] Pull all spent tomato plants — if any blight was present, bag and trash (do NOT compost)
- [ ] Add 2 inches of compost to all cleared beds; lightly till in
- [ ] **Plant cover crop** (winter rye or annual rye) on empty beds — protects soil over winter,
  adds organic matter in spring when tilled under
- [ ] Keep row cover on hand for snap peas, kale, and spinach (extend their season 2–4 weeks)
- [ ] Carrot check — Danvers carrots can stay in ground until a hard freeze (below 25°F)

---

### OCTOBER

#### Week 28 — October 1–7: Final Harvests
> *First frost possible. Keep an eye on forecasts every night.*

- [ ] Cover fall snap peas, kale, and spinach with row cover if frost predicted (extends season significantly)
- [ ] Harvest remaining peppers and any last tomatoes
- [ ] **Harvest carrots** before a hard freeze — roots split or become pithy after severe cold
- [ ] Garlic should be mulched and established
- [ ] Cucumber and zucchini: pull at first hard frost, compost healthy plants

#### Week 29 — October 8–14: Pre-Freeze Final Push
> *Average first frost: October 10–15 for Dallas PA. Hard freeze possible by mid-October.*

- [ ] **Final snap pea harvest** — pull plants after a killing frost
- [ ] **Kale and spinach** can remain — both survive down to ~20°F with some protection
- [ ] Garlic: mulch should be 4–6 inches; add more if needed
- [ ] Pull all frost-killed plants; compost healthy material; trash any diseased material
- [ ] Strawberry bed: once plants go dormant and temps consistently drop below 40°F at night,
  **mulch crowns with 6 inches of clean straw** for winter protection

#### Week 30 — October 15+ : Put the Garden to Bed
> *Season over. The work you do now sets up next year.*

- [ ] Clear all remaining plant debris (disease prevention over winter)
- [ ] **Top-dress all beds with 2–3 inches of compost** — let winter freeze-thaw cycles work it in
- [ ] Cover crop should be germinating in empty beds
- [ ] Garlic is established underground — no more watering needed until spring
- [ ] **Bring in any garden tools, hoses, and irrigation equipment** before hard freeze
- [ ] Notes and journal entries: what worked, what didn't, what to plant where next year
  (plan crop rotation — never plant tomatoes/peppers in same spot more than 2 years running)
- [ ] **Order seeds for next year** during winter sales (December–February)

## Dallas PA Soil Prep Notes
- **Back Mountain soils** are typically loamy-clay from glacial deposits — good water retention but needs drainage help
- **Amend annually** with 2–3 inches of compost worked into the top 8 inches
- **Carrot beds** need 12 inches of loose, rock-free, finely worked soil — use a raised bed or deep-till bed for best results
- **Strawberries** prefer slightly acidic soil (pH 5.5–6.5) — they'll likely thrive in unamended PA soil
- **Tomatoes and peppers** prefer pH 6.0–6.8 — lime if soil pH is below 6.0
- **Snap peas** fix their own nitrogen — inoculate seeds with rhizobium powder before sowing for best results
- Contact **Penn State Extension — Luzerne County** for local soil test kits and personalized advice
